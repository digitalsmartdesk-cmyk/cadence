Chapter I  ·  Test of Tempo

How you move through a day.

Forced Choice
When the morning is yours to spend, you’d rather…
Begin slowly and finish late.
    You trust the warm-up. Momentum is something you earn, not summon.
Scale 1–7
How often do you finish what you started this week?
3 — rarely, but it doesn’t bother me.
    Completion isn’t your unit of meaning. You measure in attention spent, not items closed.
Open Field
The last thing that made you lose track of time.
“Rearranging the bookshelf by colour, then by mood.”
    Your absorption tilts toward arrangement — ordering systems for their own sake, not for anyone else’s use.
Either / Or
A long task — you’d prefer to do it…
In one uninterrupted sitting.
    You distrust the seam. Breaks cost more to you than fatigue does.
Ranked Triplet
Order these by how you’d rather spend a quiet hour: read, walk, write.
Walk · Read · Write.
    Thinking, for you, happens at the speed of footsteps — not page or pen.
 
Chapter II  ·  Test of Threshold

Where you draw the line.
Forced Choice
Asked for a favour you can’t do well, you’d rather…
Say no, and explain nothing.
   You treat refusal as complete — not as the opening line of a negotiation.
Scale 1–7
How comfortable are you sitting with someone else’s disappointment?
5 — more than I used to be.
   Something recent taught you that other people’s feelings aren’t emergencies. The lesson is holding.
Open Field
A thing you used to tolerate and no longer will.
“Conversations where I’m the only one curious.”
   You’ve stopped subsidising attention. Reciprocity is becoming a hard requirement.
Either / Or
A boundary works best when it’s…
Quiet and consistent.
   You believe in the slow accrual of evidence over the single announced rule.
Multiple Choice
Which costs you most?
Being misunderstood by someone close.
   Proximity raises the stakes for you, not lowers them. Intimacy is where the bruises register.
 
Chapter III  ·  Test of Mirror

What you see when you look.
Open Field
One word a stranger has used about you that stuck.
“Watchful.”
   You collect the room before you enter it. Most people don’t notice this; the one who did is the one you remembered.
Forced Choice
You’d rather be seen as…
Interesting, not impressive.
   Status, to you, is a weaker reward than being asked a second question.
Scale 1–7
How accurately does your inner monologue match what you say out loud?
4 — about half of it makes it through.
   You edit in real time. Whether that’s tact or self-protection depends on the day.
Either / Or
The version of yourself you trust most is the one…
Alone at the end of the day.
   Solitude is where your verdicts get made. The morning you is provisional.
Open Field
A compliment you’ve never quite believed.
“You’re very patient.”
   From the inside, what reads as patience is something closer to deferral. You know the difference. They don’t.
 
Chapter IV  ·  Test of Compass

What you’re pointed at.
Forced Choice
If you had to choose now: depth, or breadth?
Depth.
   You’d rather know one thing too well than ten things adequately. This is a preference, not a virtue.
Open Field
A thing you want that you don’t usually admit to.
“To be the one people quote.”
   The recognition you actually want is for the way you put things — not for what you do.
Scale 1–7
How close is your life now to what you imagined at twenty?
2 — barely recognisable, and that’s fine.
   You’ve stopped grading your present against an earlier draft. Most people never manage this.
Either / Or
A good life is closer to…
A few rooms, fully lived in.
   You’d rather wear out a small life than tour a large one.
Ranked Triplet
Order these by what you’d protect first: time, attention, reputation.
Attention · Time · Reputation.
   You guard the inside more carefully than the outside. Reputation, you suspect, mostly takes care of itself.
 
Chapter V  ·  Test of Cadence

The shape of your repeating.
Open Field
Something you’ve done at least a hundred times this year.
“Re-read the same three pages before sleep.”
   Ritual, for you, isn’t about progress — it’s a way of arriving back at yourself.
Forced Choice
A habit you’d defend in court…
Walking home the long way.
   You build margin into your transitions. The detour is doing important work.
Scale 1–7
How easily do you change your mind when given good reason?
6 — quickly, and without a fuss.
   You’ve untangled your identity from your positions. Rare, and probably worked-for.
Either / Or
Change, for you, tends to come…
Slowly, and then all at once.
   You let pressure accumulate underneath the surface, then act decisively when you finally do.
Open Field
Where, in your week, are you most recognisably yourself?
“Sunday evening, kitchen, no music.”
   Your home self is a quiet self. Anyone who has only met you elsewhere has met a costume.
